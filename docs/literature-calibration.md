# Literature calibration and comparison ledger

## Scope and reading standard

This ledger separates two evidence depths instead of treating every citation as a full-paper review. The retained reading ledger records **21 unique complete-main-text readings** across distributed systems, incremental maintenance, and adjacent methods; NetChain appears in two thematic groups. These are inherited literature notes, not a new TOSEM acceptance assessment. Each received a main-text pass from an official or author-hosted version covering the design, correctness or performance argument, evaluation, limitations, conclusion, and claim-carrying figures/tables. “Complete-main-text reading” does not mean independent reproduction of every appendix, artifact, or experiment. Those passes were retained from 2026-09-11.

The manuscript bibliography was subsequently expanded to **67 scholarly references**. The additional 46 records were checked against an official publication page, DOI record, proceedings index, or primary preprint and were used only for claim-level positioning. They are **not** relabeled as complete-paper readings. `reference-audit.csv` records the category, precise relevance, verification depth, and stable source for every citation; its final record audit date is 2026-09-18. `reference-verification.csv` separately records complete author lists and the DOI or official scholarly/venue record used for every key. Big Code Search remains a recent first-author bridge from FaCoY’s first author, but as a survey it is context rather than a completed-research ancestor for the protocol.

## Main finding from the comparison

Across the 21 unique complete-paper calibration set and the 46 additional claim-level publication records, we did not identify a source that documents the exact combination evaluated here: a stateful, client-held ranked candidate buffer; an explicit lower bound for every omitted live item; a query-independent ordered update feed shared by standing queries; and repair of only those shards whose bound can still cross the global top-k threshold at a declared vector cut. The ingredients have strong precedents. Threshold stopping is classical; distributed top-k monitoring maintains remote constraints; Pequod, Noria, differential dataflow, and MuCache maintain or cache derived state; eSearch federates retrieval; and Eiger, Scalog, Skybridge, and WriteGuards make version, order, ownership, or freshness boundaries explicit. The contribution claim is therefore about the particular continuation invariant, its state/communication tradeoff, and its failure-closed composition—not invention of caching, incremental maintenance, top-k bounds, or version vectors.

The earlier one-shot prefix-plus-delta design remains an adverse result. In the co-resident model it sends more rows than an exact overlay and has no demonstrated advantage. The new design changes the information placement: the coordinator retains validated per-query state and consumes a shared event stream, so repeated queries do not re-fetch ranked prefixes. Cold queries and workloads without reuse remain outside the claimed win.

## Claim boundary after calibration

The calibrated novelty statement is intentionally narrow: **validated continuation state turns complete ordered updates into exact standing-query answers without re-querying every shard; only a shard whose omitted-row bound can affect global top-k is repaired.** The manuscript does not claim novel top-k mathematics, general incremental computation, causal snapshots, semantic code search, authenticated malicious-server proofs, or universal communication optimality. The evidence supports a bounded positive communication/state tradeoff and a reproducible failure-closed prototype; it does not by itself settle acceptance by the target journal.
